How to get longer trills ▾
  • Breathe low. Let your belly and lower ribs expand as you breathe in. Keep your shoulders still.
  • Fill up comfortably, not to bursting. A huge gasp of air creates tension and the air rushes out.
  • Steady, gentle airflow. You need just enough air to keep the lips buzzing.
  • Loose lips and jaw. If your lips stall, press a fingertip lightly into each cheek beside the corners of your mouth.
  • Quality first. A smooth 8 seconds is better practice than a wobbly 15.
  • Rest between goes, and stop if you feel light-headed.

These are general tips. Where your teacher says something different, go with her.

Chest voice

Your everyday speaking voice: lower, fuller, with a buzz in your chest.

  1. Put a hand flat on your breastbone.
  2. Say a relaxed “uh-huh” or a “hey!” like you’re calling a friend across the room. Feel the buzz.
  3. Count 1 to 5 out loud in that voice.
  4. Sing “mah” on the note below, keeping the same speech-like feel.

Head voice

Lighter and higher. It feels like it sits in your face and head rather than your chest.

  1. Put a hand on top of your head, or fingertips on your cheekbones.
  2. Do a soft owl “hoo”, or a little surprised “whoo!”.
  3. Say “hello” in a high, gentle voice, like talking to a baby.
  4. Sing “oo” on the note below. Keep it light. No pushing.

Switch between them

Say “Hello, how are you?” in chest voice, then the same in head voice. Go back and forth three times.

Siren tips

  • Let it get lighter as you go up. Don’t push volume at the top.
  • A crack or flip is normal. That’s where chest voice hands over to head voice. It’s useful information, not a mistake.
  • Keep it smooth, like a slide, not steps.
  • Start small. Only go bigger when medium feels easy.

Tips for live mode

  • Find a quiet room. Background noise confuses the meter.
  • Hum or sing “oo”, about an arm’s length from the phone.
  • Watch the line and slide. If you’re below the band, slide up gently until you’re in it. Then try to stay there.
  • Octaves count. If your voice is an octave below the target, it still counts as a match.

Ladders and tunes

Start with the 5-note ladder (“do re mi fa so” and back). Move on when it feels easy. Echo mode leaves a gap after each note, or each phrase in a tune, for you to sing it back.

Tips for matching a note ▾
  • Hum first. It takes words out of the way.
  • Hum along while it plays before trying it alone.
  • Slide into it. Start a little low and slide slowly up until it locks in.
  • Listen for the wobble. Slightly-off notes beat against each other. Matched notes blend.
  • An octave lower or higher still counts.
  • “Not sure” is a fair answer. Hearing is the skill you’re building.
